# Head of Security: hiring rubric

Source: Slack channel C0B6ES4GVM1, 2026-06-12 (thread `C0B6ES4GVM1:1781235002.295259`). Company size ~324 people.

1. **Veteran generalist.** Broad security career. Not a narrow specialist or pure compliance officer.
2. **Technical depth-on-demand.** Cloud/AWS IAM, appsec/OWASP, IR. Can engage SRE on the merits and level up the team.
3. **Tatari-stack fit.** AWS + Databricks + Google Workspace. PII/data and adtech surface.
4. **Player-coach.** Still a hands-on IC. Not a never-touch-console exec.
5. **People-developer.** Has grown engineers. Led a small team, not huge-org-only.
6. **Pragmatic, startup-stage judgment.** Risk-based tradeoffs. SOC 2 as a floor. Scrappy environments.
7. **GRC / SOC 2.** Can own it but keeps it lean.
8. **Right-level.** Director/Head at ~324 people. Does not need a CISO/VP empire.
9. **Red flags.** Compliance-only, framework-speak, IT-empire instinct, over-leveled exec, job-hopping, ego.

## Reviewer lessons (same thread)

- Resumes often echo the same JD nearly verbatim and look AI-polished. Keyword match is low signal.
- A clear career timeline matters. An employer on the resume but absent from LinkedIn, or overlapping full-time roles, is a stop signal.
- Read the order of the candidate's top accomplishments. A list led by roadmap, SOC 2, and questionnaires with application security absent is a gap.
- IT ownership is deliberately out of the role (moved out of security). A candidate who runs IT must accept that.
- Domain match (adtech, programmatic TV) is a plus, not a substitute for hands-on depth.
